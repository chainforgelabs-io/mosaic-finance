# Family Law & Divorce Impact on Financial Planning
## Finova AI Knowledge Module | Source: CSI FPSU Ch. 6 (2020)

> **INJECTION TRIGGER:** Load this module when user indicates divorce, separation, or relationship breakdown during the fact-find, or when generating plan sections for clients with blended families or support obligations.

## FUNDAMENTAL ASPECTS

### Marriage
- Creates legal obligations and property rights between spouses
- Common-law relationships recognized in most provinces after a specified cohabitation period (varies by province: 1–3 years)
- Common-law partners may have different property rights than married couples depending on province

### Divorce
- Governed by federal Divorce Act (Canada-wide) plus provincial family law legislation
- Only legally married couples divorce; common-law couples separate
- Grounds: breakdown of marriage (1 year separation, adultery, or cruelty)
- Divorce creates financial obligations: property division, support payments, pension splitting

### Custody and Access
- **Joint custody**: both parents share decision-making (most common)
- **Sole custody**: one parent has decision-making authority
- **Shared parenting / equal time**: roughly equal time with each parent — affects child support calculation
- Access parent retains right to visit and obtain information about child

## FINANCIAL ISSUES IN FAMILY LAW

### Financial Disclosure
- Both parties have duty of full and frank financial disclosure
- Must disclose: all income, assets, debts, and financial obligations
- Failure to disclose can invalidate agreements
- Financial advisor may be asked to help compile disclosure

### Child Support
- Governed by **Federal Child Support Guidelines** (table amounts based on payor's income and number of children)
- **Section 3 support**: base table amount (mandatory — not discretionary)
- **Section 7 expenses**: extraordinary expenses shared proportionally to income (childcare, medical/dental, education, extracurricular)
- Child support is: **not tax-deductible** by payor, **not taxable income** to recipient
- Continues until child is no longer a "child of the marriage" (age of majority or longer if in full-time education, illness, or disability)
- Income over $150,000: table amount applies to first $150,000; court has discretion above that

### Spousal Support
- **Compensatory**: compensates for economic disadvantage arising from marriage (e.g., career sacrifice for childcare)
- **Non-compensatory**: addresses need and standard of living established during marriage
- Governed by **Spousal Support Advisory Guidelines (SSAG)** — not law but widely used
- SSAG provides range (low, mid, high) for amount and duration based on:
  - Length of marriage
  - Income differential
  - Presence of children
- Spousal support: **tax-deductible** by payor, **taxable income** to recipient (if periodic — not lump sum)
- Can be varied if material change in circumstances

### Calculating Support Amounts
- Child support calculated first (non-discretionary)
- Spousal support calculated second (considers child support already being paid)
- Software tools commonly used: ChildView, DivorceMate
- "With child" formula vs. "without child" formula under SSAG

## PROPERTY ISSUES ON RELATIONSHIP BREAKDOWN

### Equalization of Net Family Property (Ontario Model — Most Provinces Similar)
1. Calculate each spouse's **Net Family Property (NFP)**:
   - Value of all assets on date of separation
   - MINUS value of all debts on date of separation
   - MINUS value of assets brought into marriage (with exceptions)
   - MINUS value of gifts/inheritances received during marriage (with exceptions)
2. Spouse with higher NFP pays the other spouse **half the difference**
3. **Matrimonial home** is treated specially — value at marriage is NOT deducted (full value included in NFP regardless of when acquired)

### Key Property Concepts
- **Excluded property**: gifts, inheritances, insurance proceeds, tort damages — IF kept separate and traceable
- **Tracing**: burden of proof is on the person claiming exclusion
- **Valuation date**: date of separation (not divorce)
- **Pensions**: included in NFP calculation; can be divided at source or through equalization payment

### Matrimonial Home
- Special status in most provinces
- Both spouses have equal right to possession regardless of title
- Cannot be sold, mortgaged, or encumbered without consent of both spouses
- Full value included in equalization (no deduction for pre-marriage value)
- Possession rights end upon divorce, not separation

### Domestic Contracts
- **Marriage contract (prenuptial agreement)**: made before or during marriage
- **Cohabitation agreement**: between common-law partners
- **Separation agreement**: upon relationship breakdown
- Must be: in writing, signed, witnessed
- Can be challenged if: no financial disclosure, no independent legal advice, unconscionable terms, or signed under duress

## IMPACT OF DIVORCE ON FINANCIAL PLAN

### Areas Requiring Immediate Review
1. **Cash flow and budget** — Two households now funded from same income. Significant budget restructuring needed.
2. **Insurance beneficiary designations** — Remove ex-spouse if no longer appropriate. Some designations may be irrevocable.
3. **Life insurance obligations** — Separation agreement may require maintaining life insurance to secure support payments.
4. **Pension division** — CPP/QPP credit splitting; employer pension division (QDRO equivalent).
5. **RRSP/TFSA transfers** — Can transfer between spouses on breakdown without tax consequence if pursuant to court order or written separation agreement.
6. **Will and estate plan** — Must be updated. Divorce may revoke bequests to ex-spouse (varies by province).
7. **Power of attorney** — Must be updated to remove ex-spouse.
8. **Tax filing status** — Changes to tax credits and deductions. May lose spousal amount, child care deduction may change.
9. **Child benefit payments (CCB)** — May be reallocated based on custody arrangement.
10. **Housing** — Mortgage qualification on single income, matrimonial home sale/buyout implications.

### Financial Planning Conversation Probes
- "Are you currently married, common-law, separated, or divorced?"
- "Do you have a marriage contract or cohabitation agreement?"
- "Are you currently paying or receiving child or spousal support?"
- "Has your ex-spouse been removed as beneficiary on your insurance and registered accounts?"
- "Has your will been updated since your separation?"

## ALTERNATIVE DISPUTE RESOLUTION

| Method | Description | Cost | Control |
|--------|-------------|------|---------|
| **Negotiation** | Direct discussion between parties (with or without lawyers) | Lowest | Highest |
| **Mediation** | Neutral third-party facilitates agreement; non-binding | Low-moderate | High |
| **Collaborative Law** | Each party has lawyer; all commit to resolving without court | Moderate | Moderate-high |
| **Arbitration** | Private decision-maker renders binding decision | Moderate-high | Low |
| **Litigation** | Court decides all issues | Highest | Lowest |

> **FINOVA APPLICATION:** The AI should not provide legal advice on divorce proceedings. When divorce/separation is identified, the plan should flag affected areas (property division, support obligations, insurance, estate documents) and recommend consultation with a family law lawyer. The financial plan should incorporate existing support obligations as fixed expenses and adjust projections accordingly.
