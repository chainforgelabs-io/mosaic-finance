## RISK MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK

### Four Methods of Dealing with Risk
1. **Risk Avoidance** — Eliminate the activity that creates the risk entirely
2. **Risk Reduction** — Take steps to reduce probability or severity of loss
3. **Risk Transfer** — Shift risk to another party (insurance is the primary mechanism)
4. **Risk Retention** — Accept the risk and self-insure (appropriate when consequence is small or probability is low)

### When Insurance Is Appropriate
Insurance is appropriate when: consequences of loss could be great, BUT probability of loss is relatively low. If both probability and consequence are high, insurance may be unavailable or prohibitively expensive.

## LIFE INSURANCE NEEDS ANALYSIS

### Capital Needs Analysis Method (Primary Framework)

**Step 1: Inventory Assets Available at Death**
- Cash and savings
- Personal life insurance (existing policies)
- Group life insurance (employer)
- CPP/QPP death benefit ($2,500 maximum)
- RPP death benefit
- RRSP assets to liquidate (if applicable)
- Investment portfolio to liquidate
- Other assets to be sold

**= Total Cash Available [A]**

**Step 2: Determine Estate Obligations at Death**
- Last expenses (funeral, legal, accounting) — typically $10,000–$20,000
- Mortgage cancellation
- Credit card balances
- Education fund (per child target)
- Emergency fund (3 months combined expenses)
- Car loan / other debts
- Capital gains taxes payable on death

**= Total Cash Needed [B]**

**Step 3: Calculate Income Needs of Survivor**
- Required after-tax monthly income
- Less: CPP/QPP survivor benefit (max ~$638/month if under 65)
- Less: CPP/QPP children's benefits (max ~$255/month per child under 18)
- Less: Other ongoing income sources

**= Net Monthly Income Required [C]**

**Step 4: Calculate Capital to Produce Income**
```
Total Capital Needed [D] = (C × 12) / discount_rate
```
Where discount_rate = after-tax, after-inflation real rate of return (commonly 2%)

**Step 5: Calculate Insurance Gap**
```
Additional Insurance Needed = [B] + [D] - [A]
```

### Important Notes on Capital Needs Analysis
- The formula replaces 100% of lost income **in perpetuity** — some clients may only need replacement to age of retirement
- The **single needs approach** focuses on specific family needs (e.g., education funding only)
- Needs change over time — review when:
  - Children pass dependency stage
  - Mortgages/debts are paid off
  - Assets increase in value (creating capital gains tax liability at death)
  - New children are born
  - Divorce/separation requires insurance as part of agreement
- CPP/QPP death benefit is a one-time payment of $2,500 (not ongoing)

## LIFE INSURANCE PRODUCT TYPES

| Type | Description | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Term** | Pure insurance for specific period (10, 20, 30 years). No cash value. Lowest initial cost. | Young families needing maximum coverage at minimum cost |
| **Permanent (Whole Life)** | Lifetime coverage with cash value accumulation. Level premiums. Forced savings component. | Estate planning, permanent insurance needs |
| **Participating** | Whole life that pays dividends from insurer's surplus. Dividends can reduce premiums, accumulate, or purchase additional coverage. | Long-term wealth building + insurance |
| **Universal Life** | Flexible premiums and death benefit. Cash value grows tax-sheltered. Investment component. | Tax-advantaged investing + insurance |
| **Variable Life** | Policyholder chooses investment funds. Cash value fluctuates with market performance. | Sophisticated investors wanting market exposure within insurance |
| **Joint Life / Second-to-Die** | Covers two lives; pays on first or second death. Second-to-die is cheaper per unit. | Couples, estate planning (second-to-die pays capital gains tax at last death) |

### Key Policy Provisions
- **10-Day Right of Rescission** — Client can cancel within 10 days of receiving policy
- **Grace Period** — Usually 30 days past premium due date before policy lapses
- **Non-forfeiture Options** — If premium payments stop: cash surrender value, reduced paid-up insurance, or extended term insurance
- **Beneficiary Designation** — Revocable (can change) vs. irrevocable (requires beneficiary consent to change)
- **Conversion Provisions** — Right to convert term to permanent without medical evidence
- **Renewability** — Right to renew term at end of term (at higher premium) without medical evidence

### Taxation of Life Insurance (Canada)
- Death benefit paid to named beneficiary: **tax-free** and bypasses estate/probate
- Cash surrender value: taxable gain = (CSV) − (ACB of policy)
- Transfer to spouse: at adjusted cost basis (tax-deferred rollover)
- Transfer to children: at fair market value (may trigger taxable gain)
- **Exempt policies**: investment income accumulates tax-free inside policy
- **Non-exempt policies**: annual investment income is taxable (accrual taxation)

## DISABILITY INSURANCE NEEDS ANALYSIS

### Three Design Components
1. **Monthly Benefit Amount** — Up to 60–75% of gross earned income. Should not exceed pre-disability income (prevents malingering incentive).
2. **Waiting Period (Elimination Period)** — 30, 60, 90, 180, or 365 days before benefits begin. Longer waiting period = lower premium. Factor in: group plan coverage, emergency reserves, EI sickness benefits (15 weeks).
3. **Benefit Period** — Length of time benefits are paid. Options: 2 years, 5 years, 10 years, to age 65. Longer = higher premium.

### Calculating Disability Coverage Need
**Method 1: Expense-Based**
Total monthly recurring expenses of household = minimum monthly benefit needed

**Method 2: Income Replacement (Insurer's Approach)**
- Start with gross earned income (salary, commissions, fees, net self-employment income)
- Apply 60–75% replacement ratio (since disability benefits are usually tax-free)
- Subtract: investment income that continues during disability
- Subtract: other disability sources (group LTD, Workers' Compensation, CPP/QPP disability)
- Subtract: EI sickness benefits (consider for waiting period offset, max 15 weeks)
- = Monthly benefit to apply for

### Definition of Disability (Critical Distinction)
| Definition | Description | Cost |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| **Own occupation** | Unable to perform duties of own specific occupation | Most expensive; most favourable to insured |
| **Regular occupation** | Unable to perform duties of own occupation for initial period (often 2 years), then "any occupation" applies | Mid-range |
| **Any occupation** | Unable to perform duties of any occupation for which reasonably suited by education/training | Least expensive; hardest to claim |

### Key Benefit Provisions
- **Waiver of Premium** — Premiums waived during disability (after waiting period)
- **Presumptive Disability** — Automatic total disability for: loss of sight, speech, hearing, or use of two limbs
- **Residual/Partial Disability** — Proportional benefit when partially disabled (based on % income loss)
- **Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA)** — Benefits increase with inflation during claim
- **Future Income Protection** — Option to increase coverage as income grows, without medical underwriting
- **Non-cancellable** — Insurer cannot cancel or change terms/premiums (most favourable)
- **Guaranteed renewable** — Insurer must renew but can increase premiums for entire class

### Government Disability Programs (Canada)
| Program | Eligibility | Benefit | Duration |
|---------|------------|---------|----------|
| **EI Sickness** | Employed, insurable hours met | Max ~55% of insurable earnings (capped) | 15 weeks |
| **CPP Disability** | Made sufficient CPP contributions; "severe and prolonged" disability | Flat rate + earnings-based component | Until recovery, age 65, or death |
| **QPP Disability** | Quebec equivalent of CPP | Similar structure | Similar |
| **Workers' Compensation** | Work-related injury/illness | 85% of take-home pay (Ontario, varies by province) | Duration of disability |

## CRITICAL ILLNESS INSURANCE

### What It Covers
- Lump-sum, tax-free payment upon diagnosis of covered critical illness
- Common covered conditions: cancer, heart attack, stroke, coronary artery bypass, kidney failure, major organ transplant, multiple sclerosis, blindness, deafness, paralysis
- Typically a 30-day survival period required after diagnosis

### Key Features
- **Return of Premium (ROP)** — Premiums refunded if no claim made (on death or at policy expiry)
- Coverage can be term or permanent
- Benefit amount typically $25,000 to $2,000,000
- Not coordinated with other insurance — full amount paid regardless of other coverage

### When to Recommend
- Self-employed without group benefits
- Clients with mortgage or significant debts
- Clients who would need to access retirement savings if seriously ill
- Supplement to disability insurance (CI covers conditions that may not trigger disability definition)

## LONG-TERM CARE (LTC) INSURANCE

### What It Covers
- Daily or monthly benefit for custodial/personal care when unable to perform Activities of Daily Living (ADLs): bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, eating
- Or when cognitive impairment requires supervision
- Covers: nursing homes, assisted living, home care, adult day care

### Key Design Elements
- **Benefit amount**: daily ($50–$300+) or monthly
- **Benefit period**: 2 years, 3 years, 5 years, lifetime
- **Waiting period**: 0–180 days
- **Inflation protection**: optional rider to increase benefits
- Benefits are generally tax-free in Canada

### When to Recommend
- Clients aged 50+ without family care support
- Clients with family history of cognitive decline
- Clients who want to protect estate/inheritance from care costs
- Provincial health insurance covers hospital and physician services but generally NOT long-term custodial care

## GENERAL INSURANCE CONSIDERATIONS

### Property & Casualty Basics
- **Homeowner's insurance**: replacement cost vs. actual cash value (replacement cost minus depreciation)
- **Automobile insurance**: liability coverage (mandatory), collision, comprehensive, uninsured motorist
- **Liability insurance**: umbrella policies for high-net-worth clients
- **Title insurance**: protects against defects in property title

### Insurance Guarantor: Assuris
- Protects Canadian policyholders if life insurance company fails
- Covers: life insurance, health insurance, annuities, segregated funds
- Coverage: minimum of $200,000 or 85% of promised benefits (whichever is higher) for life insurance death benefits

### Five Fundamental Principles of Group Insurance
1. Insurance is incidental to the group (group exists for purpose other than insurance)
2. Flow of members through the group (prevents adverse selection)
3. Minimum participation requirements
4. Third-party premium sharing (employer contributes)
5. Simple administration

### Taxation of Group Benefits
- Employer-paid life insurance premiums: taxable benefit to employee
- Employer-paid disability premiums: not a taxable benefit, BUT disability benefits received are taxable
- Employee-paid disability premiums: benefits received are tax-free
- This distinction is critical for disability needs calculations
