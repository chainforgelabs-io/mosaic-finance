## CONTRACT LAW ESSENTIALS

### Five Prerequisites of an Enforceable Contract
1. **Offer** — Clear, definite proposal capable of acceptance
2. **Acceptance** — Unconditional agreement to all terms (counteroffer = rejection + new offer)
3. **Consideration** — Something of value exchanged by both parties (money, promise, service)
4. **Capacity** — Parties must be legally competent (age of majority, mentally capable, not intoxicated)
5. **Legality** — Purpose of contract must be legal

### Discharge of Contracts
- **Performance** — Both parties fulfill obligations
- **Agreement** — Mutual agreement to end or modify
- **Frustration** — Impossible to perform due to unforeseen event
- **Breach** — One party fails to perform (gives rise to remedies)

### Remedies for Breach
- **Damages** — Monetary compensation for loss
- **Specific performance** — Court orders party to fulfill obligations (typically for unique goods/property)
- **Injunction** — Court orders party to stop doing something
- **Rescission** — Contract is cancelled; parties restored to pre-contract position

### Quebec Civil Code
- Quebec uses civil code system (not common law)
- Contracts governed by Civil Code of Quebec
- Similar principles but different terminology and procedures
- Finova should flag Quebec-specific considerations where relevant

## AGENCY LAW

### Definition
Agency is a relationship where one person (agent) acts on behalf of another (principal) with authority to affect the principal's legal relations with third parties.

### Financial Advisor as Agent
- Advisors often act as agents for clients AND for their employer/dealer
- Creates potential for **dual agency conflicts**
- Agent's duties: loyalty, obedience, skill and care, accounting, notification
- Agent must not exceed authority granted by principal

### Types of Authority
- **Express authority** — Specifically stated (written or oral)
- **Implied authority** — Reasonably necessary to carry out express authority
- **Apparent authority** — Third party reasonably believes agent has authority based on principal's actions

## BUSINESS STRUCTURES

| Structure | Liability | Taxation | Complexity | Best For |
|-----------|-----------|----------|------------|----------|
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Unlimited personal liability | Business income = personal income (taxed at personal rate) | Simplest | Low-risk businesses, freelancers, early-stage ventures |
| **General Partnership** | Unlimited personal + joint/several liability for ALL partners | Income flows through to partners proportionally (taxed at personal rate) | Simple (but need partnership agreement) | Professional practices, shared ventures |
| **Limited Partnership** | General partner: unlimited. Limited partners: limited to investment. | Income flows through to partners | Moderate | Real estate, investment pools |
| **Corporation** | Limited to corporate assets (shareholder liability limited to investment) | Corporate tax rate (small business deduction on first $500K of active business income). Dividends paid to shareholders are taxed again (integration). | Most complex | Growing businesses, income splitting, liability protection, succession planning |
| **Co-operative** | Generally limited | Taxed as corporation; surplus distributed to members as patronage dividends | Moderate | Member-owned businesses |

### Corporation — Key Tax Planning Considerations
- **Small Business Deduction**: reduced federal tax rate (~9%) on first $500,000 of active business income for CCPCs
- **Integration principle**: corporate tax + personal tax on dividends should roughly equal personal tax on same income earned directly
- **Retained earnings**: can accumulate wealth inside corporation at lower corporate rate
- **Passive investment income**: reduces access to small business deduction if over $50,000
- **Salary vs. dividends**: ongoing planning decision based on marginal rates, CPP contributions, RRSP room
- **Individual Pension Plans (IPPs)**: alternative to RRSP for incorporated business owners, especially over age 40
- **Estate freeze**: locks current value for tax purposes; future growth goes to next generation

## FIDUCIARY DUTY

### Definition
A fiduciary duty is the highest standard of care in law. The fiduciary must act in the best interest of the beneficiary, putting the beneficiary's interests ahead of their own.

### When Financial Advisors Are Fiduciaries
- Trustee of a trust
- When advisor has discretionary authority over client's investments
- When significant power imbalance exists (client relies heavily on advisor's expertise)
- When advisor holds themselves out as providing comprehensive advice
- Courts increasingly finding fiduciary duty in advisor-client relationships

### Fiduciary Obligations
- **Loyalty** — Must act solely in the interest of the client
- **Full disclosure** — Must disclose all material information, including conflicts of interest
- **Skill and competence** — Must exercise reasonable skill and care
- **Avoid conflicts** — Must not place personal interest above client's
- **Accountability** — Must maintain accurate records and account for all dealings

## CONSUMER PROTECTION IN CANADA

### Key Frameworks
- Provincial consumer protection legislation (varies by province)
- Competition Act (federal) — prohibits misleading advertising, price fixing
- Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) — federal privacy law
- Provincial privacy legislation (e.g., Saskatchewan PIPA)
- Interest Act (federal) — governs disclosure of interest rates

### Cost of Credit Disclosure
- Must disclose: total cost of borrowing, annual interest rate, all fees and charges
- Cooling-off periods for certain consumer contracts
- Unfair business practices legislation in most provinces

## BANKRUPTCY LAW

### Key Concepts for Financial Planning
- **Bankruptcy** — Legal process providing relief from debts
- **Consumer proposal** — Arrangement to pay portion of debts over time (max 5 years)
- **Exempt assets** (vary by province): principal residence equity (some provinces), necessary household goods, tools of trade, pension plans (RRSPs up to certain limits)
- **Non-dischargeable debts**: student loans (< 7 years), child/spousal support, fraud debts, court fines
- RRSP contributions made within 12 months of bankruptcy can be clawed back
- Bankruptcy stays on credit report for 6–7 years (first time)

### Financial Planning Implications
- Clients with significant debt may need to evaluate bankruptcy vs. consumer proposal vs. debt consolidation
- Insurance-based products (segregated funds, insurance policies) may have creditor protection if named beneficiary is spouse, child, grandchild, or parent
- This creditor protection feature is a planning tool for business owners and professionals with liability exposure

## TORT LAW & PROFESSIONAL LIABILITY

### Errors & Omissions Insurance (E&O)
- Mandatory for insurance advisors in most provinces
- Covers: negligence in advice, failure to act, breach of confidentiality, inadequate coverage recommendations
- Common claims: failure to recommend adequate coverage, misrepresentation of product features, failure to disclose material information
