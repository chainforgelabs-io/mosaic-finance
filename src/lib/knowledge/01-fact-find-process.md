## THE SIX-STEP PLANNING PROCESS

Financial planning follows two activities across six steps:

### Activity 1: Analyze the Client's Situation
1. **Establish the client-advisor relationship** — Discuss approach, disclose conflicts of interest, set expectations for what the plan will cover and what information is needed.
2. **Collect data and information** — Gather both qualitative and quantitative data (see frameworks below). Information must be complete and accurate.
3. **Analyze data and information** — Prepare net worth statement, income/expense statement, preliminary budget, insurance needs analysis, retirement needs analysis, tax projections, investment portfolio analysis. Identify gaps, inconsistencies, and unrealistic goals.

### Activity 2: Implement the Financial Plan
4. **Recommend strategies to meet goals** — Develop tailored plan considering client's goals, values, temperament, and risk tolerance. Use assumptions for: rates of return, inflation, life expectancy, income changes, future tax rates.
5. **Implement recommendations** — Identify specific products, professional team, and action steps.
6. **Conduct periodic review** — Monitor progress, review annually at minimum, and whenever major life changes occur (birth, death, marriage, divorce, job change, illness, retirement, tax law changes).

## CLIENT DATA COLLECTION FRAMEWORK

### Qualitative Data (Captures Understanding & Attitude)
- Risk tolerance and feeling toward risk
- Financial objectives and their priority
- Lifestyle objectives
- Attitude toward financial products
- Level of financial sophistication
- Comfort with economic concepts
- Emotional relationship with money

### Quantitative Data

**Financial Information:**
- All assets (registered and non-registered)
- All liabilities (mortgage, loans, credit cards)
- Income sources (employment, investment, pension, government)
- Family expenses (fixed and variable)
- Employee benefits
- Tax situation (marginal rate, province)

**Personal Documents to Review:**
- Wills
- Pre/post-nuptial agreements
- Separation agreements
- Insurance policies
- Trust agreements

**Business/Employment Data (if applicable):**
- Financial statements
- Shareholder agreements
- Stock option plans
- Profit-sharing plans
- Employee benefit plans

## DETERMINING CLIENT OBJECTIVES

### Six Items to Establish

1. **Time Horizon** — Most important question. Different goals have different horizons. Short-term (house, car) vs. long-term (retirement). All other planning decisions, especially risk, are coloured by time horizon.

2. **Liquidity Needs** — Four common reasons:
   - Emergency cushion (3–6 months of expenses)
   - Financial goal within next 2 years
   - Known pending expenses (tax payments, equalization payments, probate)
   - Investment opportunity reserve

3. **Income vs. Growth** — Influenced by life cycle stage. Even retirees need some equity exposure to protect against inflation. Retirement income planning is critical in low-interest environments.

4. **Goal Achievement** — The "Will I have enough?" calculation. Six factors for accumulation:
   - Years to retirement
   - Annual income required at retirement
   - Existing retirement savings
   - Annual savings capacity
   - Inflation rate
   - Expected return on savings
   
   Client controls 4 of 6 (retirement age, required income, annual savings, investment mix). Only existing assets and inflation are beyond control.

5. **Tax Considerations** — Tax planning is part of the process. Dividend income has preferential treatment vs. interest. Some products (whole life, capital class funds) have inherent tax benefits. Performance should be measured after-tax.

6. **Primary and Secondary Objectives** — Clients rarely have one goal. Advisor must distinguish and prioritize. Different strategies may apply to different "pools" of money. Objectives change over time with life events.

## LIFE CYCLE STAGES

| Stage | Description | Risk Profile | Investment Focus |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Accumulation** | Just entered workforce, few assets, significant debts (mortgage, student loans). Income relatively low. Very long time horizon. | Higher risk tolerance | Maximum growth |
| **Consolidation** | Income exceeds expenses. Career established, mortgage reduced, children independent. Significant portfolio built. | Moderate risk | Balanced, slightly more fixed-income |
| **Financial Independence** | Living expenses funded by investment and pension income. No longer in workforce. Cannot make up significant losses. Time horizon may still be 20+ years. | Lower risk tolerance | Blue-chip securities, current income, purchasing power protection |
| **Gifting** | Financial assets exceed personal needs. Time horizon shifts to that of the recipient (children, charities). | Varies by recipient | Aligned to recipient's needs |

## COMPREHENSIVE VS. MODULAR PLANNING

| Approach | Description | Best For |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Comprehensive** | Thorough analysis covering all financial goals. Extensive data collection, detailed budgets, cash flow, net worth, tax summaries. Long-term horizon. | High-net-worth clients, complex situations |
| **Modular / Goal-Based** | Simplified approach focusing on current or pending life-cycle stage. Addresses 1–2 primary objectives. Intermediate time horizon. Defers other goals to future updates. | Most clients, especially accumulation/consolidation stage |

> **MOSAIC APPLICATION:** The modular approach maps directly to Mosaic Finance's tiered product — Essential tier users get a modular plan focused on their 1–2 most pressing goals; Pro/Premium users can receive more comprehensive analysis over time through quarterly re-plans.

## UNREALISTIC GOALS — RESOLUTION FRAMEWORK

| Unrealistic Goal | Possible Adjustments |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Retire at given age | Increase timeframe / retire later, reduce retirement income needs, increase return (accept more risk), reduce current spending |
| Save a specified amount | Cut discretionary expenses, cut fixed expenses, increase income (second earner), use forced savings |
| Finance education | Student loans, part-time/summer employment, RESP with CESG |

## LETTER OF ENGAGEMENT REQUIREMENTS

The formal client-advisor contract must specify:
- Range of services to be performed
- Method of compensation
- Client's responsibility in the planning process
- Client's responsibility for compensating other professionals (accountant, lawyer)
- 5-business-day cancellation provision with full refund of advance planning fee
- Authorization for Information form signed by client

## ETHICS FRAMEWORK (PFP / FP CANADA)

Eight principles that govern financial planning professionals:

1. **Loyalty** — Client's interests first; disclose and mitigate conflicts
2. **Integrity** — Adherence to moral rules of honesty and justice
3. **Objectivity** — Intellectual honesty, impartiality, sound judgment
4. **Competence** — Attain and maintain high knowledge and skill
5. **Fairness** — Provide what clients should reasonably expect; disclose all relevant facts
6. **Confidentiality** — Protect client information; access only to authorized parties
7. **Diligence** — Due care and prudence in handling client affairs; timely and thorough
8. **Professionalism** — Conduct that inspires confidence and respect

> **MOSAIC APPLICATION:** These principles should be embedded in the AI's conversational tone and reflected in the mandatory disclaimer language. The human professional review layer is the structural enforcement mechanism.
